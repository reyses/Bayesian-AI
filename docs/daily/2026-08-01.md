## 1. `warnstop <price>` — hard stop → warning

**What changed.** New command in
[pocket_dojo.py](../../research/dojo_forge/tools/pocket_dojo.py). Stores the
level as `pos['warn_stop']`, **deliberately not `pos['stop']`**, so `_advance`
can never see or act on it. The step loop halts at 5s precision, leaves the
position open, and logs an `alarm` event with `kind=WARN-STOP`.

**Why (owner).** *"if we make the -10 pt stop a warning also? since in theroy
we are doing this to distall and generalize for a ML, so it should be able to
handle the split second desition."*

The argument is sound and worth keeping: **a hard stop generates zero training
signal.** It fires, and the corpus records nothing about what the human would
have done. As a warning it produces exactly the (state → decision) pairs the
student model needs, at the hardest moments.

**Counterpoint on record.** Today the hard stop was *right* — the −10.89 capped
what became an 18pt+ excursion. If warn-stops teach a model that overrides are
free, that is a dangerous lesson. The resolution is inside the owner's own
idea: the corpus lets us **measure** whether overrides beat the mechanical
stop. Self-resolving — *provided we do not distill override behaviour until
that number exists.*

**Standing split:** WARN in the dojo (harvest decisions), HARD in live (bound
risk), until the data says otherwise.

Also chose `warn_stop` as the key name specifically to be structurally immune
to the earlier double-count bug class (a position whose stop lives in the state
dict must never also be hand-managed).

## 2. Alarm-halt lookahead — real bug, fixed

**The flaw.** `_advance` commits the **whole 1m bar** before the 5s alarm check
runs. So an alarm halting at 09:56:40 still rendered the complete minute — the
run past the level to 19697.75 *and* the fade back to 19684.75 — while asking
the owner to decide *at* 19684.25. That is lookahead inside the decision frame,
exactly what the fog exists to prevent, and it would have poisoned every
decision logged off an alarm halt.

Caught before anything was logged from the affected frame. Owner agreed with
the diagnosis and noted that particular frame was harmless (no position, no
decision taken — a missed opportunity, not a contaminated label).

**The fix.** `s['halt_ts5']` records the 5s timestamp of the touch.
`_render` then sets `ts_cut_now` to it — which truncates the cubic, the wick
strip and both telescope panels automatically, since they all key off that one
cutoff — and rebuilds the halt bar's main-panel 1m OHLC from its own 5s bars
(the only series read straight from `df`). `halt_ts5` is cleared on any fresh
`step`.

`peek` was taught to walk **forward from the halt point** rather than jumping
to the bar close: `peek 10` advances the truncated frame 10s and stays
truncated; a peek that overruns the bar end spends what is left of the bar and
carries the remainder into normal `peek_offset` semantics.

**Verified** by rewinding to bar 954 and re-running the alarm:

| bar 956 | O | H | L | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full (old, leaky) | 19670.25 | **19697.75** | 19661.00 | **19684.75** |
| truncated at 09:56:40 (new) | 19670.25 | **19689.25** | 19661.00 | **19689.25** |

`peek 10` → halt frame walked to 09:56:50, still truncated.
`peek 30` → overran bar end, cleared halt, carried 25s into `peek_offset`.
Live state restored to bar 956 afterwards.

## 5. `prevday [N]` rebuilt + `month [res]` — multi-session context views

**Owner asks, in sequence.** *"I want to see the look back of the full
previous day"* → *"the current bar we are at, and the previous 4d"* →
*"when I use the prevday [N] command I want to see the 4 prev days with the
current day up to the current bar"* → *"now let's render the month"*.

**`prevday [N]`** (default N=4) — N completed sessions **plus today up to
`cur`** as one continuous chart. Day separators, per-day RTH shading, each
day's high/low as a **day-width segment** (N full-width lines are unreadable);
only the last completed session's extremes/settle and NOW get full-width lines.

**`month [res]`** — month-to-date at 15m default. Only files of `s['day']`'s
own month **up to and including today**; later files exist on disk and must
never be drawn (straight lookahead).

**Causality, one cutoff for all resolutions.** `_render_prevday(s, back, res,
label)` computes a single cutoff timestamp — the alarm halt point if set, else
the current 1m bar's close — filters every source to `ts <= cutoff`, and
rebuilds the trailing **partial** bar from 5s. So a coarser view can never
smuggle in future bars, and the last 15m bar shows only what had printed.
Verified: `prevday` at 1m reproduces its pre-refactor output exactly.

**Axis fix (`_draw_tele_panel`).** A 1h panel has `ts % 3600 == 0` on *every*
bar, so all 96 got a gridline **and** a rotated `%m/%d %H:%M` label — the
4d/1h view rendered as unreadable mush. Capped at 14 evenly-spaced marks; bars
unchanged, annotation density only.

`_send()` generalised to accept an optional path (was hardcoded to `PNG`).

## 6. CONTRACT ROLL — owner caught it; §5's cross-day analysis retracted

**Owner:** *"I think we landed on a day of a contract stich (see the big
jump)."* He read it off the chart. He was right.

`DATA/ATLAS/roll_manifest.csv`:

```
2024_09_13  MNQU4
2024_09_16  MNQZ4   <- the dojo session's day
```

2024_09_16 is one of exactly **four** roll days in 2024 (03/11, 06/17, 09/16,
12/16) and the replay landed on it. ATLAS day files are per-day *chosen
outrights*, so every multi-session view splices two instruments at that seam —
and §5 read levels straight across it.

### Retracted
- "19684.75 is 1.75pt from the month origin 19683" — a coincidence between two
  **different contracts**. The headline finding of §5 is dead.
- "the flush poked 16.25pt into Friday's range" — 19577 is U4.
- "+157pt gap over Friday's high" — that is the **calendar spread**, not a gap.
- "1240pt 4-session advance", "160% of prior range" — spliced spans.
- Penetration compression: 329 → 108 → 87 are U4-internal and **survive**;
  today's 16.25 cannot join them. n=3, weaker than the already-weak n=4.

### Survives
Everything same-contract and same-day (Z4): the 09:35 low 19560.75, the +26pt
5s rip, the 19684.25 consolidation ceiling, the spike-vs-grind read. All
intraday calls stand.

### Fix — `_contracts()` + roll guard
New `_contracts(day_list)` reads the manifest; `_render_prevday` detects any
contract change inside the rendered span, draws it as a bold purple rule
("levels do NOT cross this line", label flips to right-align when the roll sits
near the right edge), and **leads the caption** with `*** CONTRACT ROLL INSIDE
THIS SPAN … cross-day levels spanning it are INVALID ***`. A clean span prints
its contract instead, so the absence of a warning is now itself informative.
Missing manifest returns `{}` rather than pretending the span is clean.

**Manifest note:** the `rolled` and `calendar_fallback` columns are `False` on
every row of the entire file — dead flags. Roll transitions are encoded *only*
by `chosen` changing between consecutive days. Any future consumer must diff
`chosen`, not trust `rolled`.

**Not offered:** U4 settle 19504.00 → Z4 open 19734.25 = +230.25, which mixes
the calendar spread with the weekend move. It cannot be decomposed without both
contracts quoted, so no spread-shifted level was given to the owner.

### Process lesson
I built three multi-session views and delivered increasingly confident
cross-day structure off all of them without once checking what instrument the
bars belonged to. The manifest was sitting in `DATA/ATLAS/`. **Any tool that
stitches ATLAS day files must check the roll manifest before any cross-day
claim is made from it.**

## 7. Owner pushback — translate roll levels, don't discard them

**Owner:** *"Umm I do disagree a human would still look at the prior levels
for structure and make decisions on it."*

He is right and my §6 conclusion ("on a roll day you have no usable prior-day
levels") was the lazy answer. Both contracts track the same index — the

a translation, not a discard.

### New tool: `research/dojo_forge/tools/roll_spread.py`

ATLAS never quotes both contracts side by side, so the spread cannot be read
directly. But every session boundary yields an (open − prior settle) jump, and
roll boundaries separate from the rest by a mile:

| boundary | contracts | jump | z vs control |
|---|---|---|---|
| 03/08→03/11 | MNQH4→MNQM4 | +245.00 (+1.357%) | +15.5 |
| 06/14→06/17 | MNQM4→MNQU4 | +258.50 (+1.312%) | +15.0 |
| **09/13→09/16** | **MNQU4→MNQZ4** | **+230.25 (+1.181%)** | **+13.5** |
| 12/13→12/16 | MNQZ4→MNQH5 | +292.50 (+1.344%) | +15.4 |

Control: 254 non-roll boundaries, mean `+0.0118%`, sd `0.0866%`.

`spread% ≈ roll jump% − control mean%`; the CI is the **control sd**, i.e. the
overnight move that cannot be separated out. Four rolls a year is far too few
to bootstrap the roll side — the honest uncertainty is "how much can a weekend
move", so that is what is reported.

**Per-roll, not pooled**, deliberately: the spread is carry, so it tracks rate
expectations. 2024_09_16 is the *lowest* of the four (+1.181%) — exactly what
maximum priced-in easing should do, and the Fed cut 50bp two days later.
Pooling would wash that signal out.

**Result for this session: spread ≈ +227.9pt, 95% [+194.8, +261.1].**

## 8. "What's our best guess for the next 20 minutes?" — measured, not guessed

analog of the live setup and reports the empirical forward distribution.

**Analog definition** (all causal at the decision instant): ≥20pt inside 15s of

starting 09:45–10:15 ET. Anchor = the giveback point. `--exclude 2024_09_16`,
since reading its own forward bars is lookahead.

**N = 291 analogs across 291 sessions** — one per session (the 30-min ET window
makes the cooldown redundant and the samples independent). The setup occurs on
roughly half of all sessions: common, not exotic.

### Direction — there is none
- mean **+4.96pt**, 95% CI **[−3.94, +14.14]** → **not significant**
- median +7.25pt, **52.6%** continue in the spike direction
- quantiles p10 −80.8 / p25 −40.5 / p50 +7.2 / p75 +51.4 / p90 +89.5

### Movement — there is a lot
- MFE median **50.8pt** (p75 84.0) · MAE median **45.5pt** (p75 80.0)
- **73.9%** of paths touch both +10 and −10 inside the window
- at ±20: 27.5% favorable-only, 19.2% adverse-only, 52.9% both

### Mapped onto price from 19684.75
| | price | nearest translated level |
|---|---|---|
| median MFE +50.8 | 19735.55 | Fri settle-equiv **19732** |
| median MAE −45.5 | 19639.25 | Fri low-equiv **19624** |

The measured 20-minute envelope lands almost exactly on the two translated

### Correction issued: the 29% figure was the wrong instrument
I had told the owner "spike shape → 29% resume." That number comes from the
**pullback hazard surface**, conditioned on 35–75% retrace depth *after a 2–5
minute stall inside a leg*. The live case gave back 38% **in 15 seconds** —
none of that conditioning applies. Same failure class as the direction-latency
probe (right harness, wrong question). The correct figure for this setup is
**52.6%, i.e. no edge**.

Wide two-sided chop, roughly 19640–19735, no directional edge, ~50/50 on the

direction; even ±20 sees 53% touch both sides. **Verdict: pass, not a trade.**

This is the same lesson as the fail-safe placement work — a stop inside the
noise distribution stops being a fail-safe and becomes the exit mechanism.

## 9. Sigma-extreme ENTRY — tested, NULL

**Owner:** *"the NMP strategy is to ride the chop, by using the sigma extremes,
or other option, but the concept is the same way the +-10p but in our favor."*

Split into two claims before testing:

- **Exit half — already dead.** "±10 in our favor" is the bracket mirrored.
  Brackets lost at every width/asymmetry over 250k trades; optional stopping
  (`P(hit −S before +T) = T/(S+T)`) makes gross EV identically zero for *any*
  exit pair on a martingale, so flipping sides changes nothing. The 74%
  both-touch does not imply fading pays — it means small wins 74% of the time
  against a p10 of −80pt.
- **Entry half — genuinely untested.** Optional stopping constrains exits only.
  "Enter at a sigma extreme" is the separate claim that price is not a
  martingale *conditional on being stretched*. Standing hint: the −3σ
  breakout-FOLLOW variant tested at 35.7% win — a clean 2:1 inversion pointing
  at the fade.

### New tool: `research/dojo_forge/tools/sigma_fade_test.py`
Entry-only. No stop/target/trail — mixing exits in is how the earlier T×N sweep
manufactured phantom edge. Cubic endpoint on 5s window 90 (deployed spec),
residual sigma over 20min, RTH only, **edge-triggered** touches (level-triggered
inflated N ~10× in a prior bug). Control = random entries from the **same
sessions and clock window**, so time-of-day volatility cannot pose as a sigma
effect. `--exclude 2024_09_16`.

### Result — 603 sessions, ~88k touches, control N=21,384

18 cells (3σ × 2 sides × 3 horizons). **One** significant:

| entry | N | mean | Δ vs control 95% CI | median | win% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −2σ FADE long, 5min | 27,766 | **+0.48pt** | `[+0.03, +0.92]` | +1.00 | 52.6% |

**Not a trade, for two independent reasons:**
1. One hit in 18 tests at α=0.05 is exactly the chance expectation (~0.9 false
   positives), and the CI clears zero by 0.03pt.
2. **+0.48pt against 0.89pt friction** — the edge is *half* the cost of
   capturing it. It loses money while being statistically real.

**Confound checked:** "buying dips" could just be long exposure in an up year.
Long-only random RTH entry, 5min: **+0.097pt**, CI `[−0.318, +0.517]`, 50.6%
win — not significant. So the sigma-specific component is genuinely +0.38pt,
not drift. Real, and still worthless.

### The one interesting non-tradeable finding
The long/short asymmetry is consistent across **every** sigma and **every**
horizon:
- below the cubic (fade long): 51.6–52.8% win, positive means
- above the cubic (fade short): 48.4–49.8% win, ~zero/negative

Dips revert slightly; pops do not. Six rows leaning the same way. (Horizons
overlap, so these are not independent confirmations — but the sign consistency
across σ levels is worth recording.) The instrument is **not symmetric**.

### Two conclusions worth carrying forward
1. **Sigma extremes do not discriminate.** Add to the list of killed mechanical
   deciders: flip-on-turn, brackets, ratchet-as-strategy, commitment
   thresholds, cut policies, and now sigma-extreme entry. The pattern is
   unbroken — every mechanical **decider** loses; every layer used as
   **instrumentation** works. The owner's own formulation keeps holding.
2. **An inverted signal is not a free edge on the other side.** The 35.7%
   follow-result did *not* translate into a profitable fade. Worth remembering
   before chasing the next inversion.

## 10. Oscillation harvesting — SIGNAL CONFIRMED, TRADE LOSES

**Owner:** *"let's test it out, the fun part is that we needed to enter and wait
until we hit the oslialation point and then start osilation harnessing."*

Distinct from §9: that entered at an extreme *unconditionally*. This waits for
the regime to **identify itself** — trade only after K oscillations are already
observed. It refuses to predict, which sidesteps the 0.57-AUC
oscillator/runaway wall entirely.

### Tool: `research/dojo_forge/tools/oscillation_harvest_test.py`
Traverse = z crosses from ±1.5σ through to the opposite band (cubic 5s w90, σ
20min), edge-triggered, RTH only. At each completion K = traverses in the prior
30min; fade toward the opposite band; stop **20pt** (outside the fakeout
distribution, p75 room 24.8pt, so the fail-safe stays a fail-safe); max hold
60min; friction 0.89pt charged per attempt.

**539 sessions · 54,911 attempts.**

### The signal is REAL
P(next traverse completes) by K: 63.6% → 66.3% → 70.9% → 75.0% → 78.6% →
79.4% (K=0..5), **+16pp, monotone, huge N.** Chop begets chop. The owner's
instinct was right and I would not have bet on it.

### The trade LOSES at every K

| K | N | complete | mean net | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 558 | 63.6% | −1.12 | [−2.68, +0.44] |
| 1 | 612 | 66.3% | −0.47 | [−2.01, +1.16] |
| 2 | 964 | 70.9% | −0.95 | [−2.14, +0.20] |
| 3–4 | 5,289 | 77.4% | −0.60 | [−1.06, −0.15] |
| 5+ | **47,488** | 78.5% | **−0.55** | **[−0.70, −0.40]** |

Pooled −0.57pt, CI [−0.71, −0.42] — **significantly negative, not a null.**
47k attempts at K≥5: not a power problem.

### The mechanism, made visible
Average win size derived from the table (`mean_net = P·W − (1−P)·20 − 0.89`;
timeouts ≈0% so the algebra is exact):

| K | win rate | avg win |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 63.6% | **11.08pt** |
| 2 | 70.9% | 8.12pt |
| 5+ | 78.5% | **5.91pt** |

**Win rate +14.9pp. Win size −47%. They cancel.**

More chop ⇒ tighter σ ⇒ the opposite band is *closer*. You hit it more often
precisely because it is worth less. The 20pt stop never moved. Confirming the
regime buys a better hit rate and charges exactly that much in payoff.

This is optional stopping again — but it is the **first time we have watched it
happen**. The theorem says win rate can be moved anywhere and EV will not
follow; this is the receipt, 55k trades wide.

### Where this leaves the idea
The regime read is a genuine, measurable fact — arguably the strongest
predictive relationship found in this program. It simply cannot be monetised by
fading the band, because **the band shrinks with the very thing being
detected**. Any use of it must run through an instrument whose payoff does
**not** scale with σ.

## 11. Sigma stop vs fixed stop — EV is invariant to stop geometry

**Owner:** *"The stratagy is either a careful watcher or a sigma stop, in this
case it would be a careful watcher (ML precursor)."*

### Sigma stop — tested (`--stop-mode sigma --stop-sigma 3.0`)
Same 539 sessions, same 54,911 entries; only the stop rule changed.

| | fixed 20pt | 3×σ |
|---|---|---|
| K≥5 win | 78.5% | **63.2%** |
| K≥5 net | −0.55pt | −0.62pt |
| pooled net | −0.57pt | −0.63pt |
| pooled 95% CI | [−0.71, −0.42] | [−0.78, −0.47] |

**Prediction was "worse" and it is worse — but the claim is not established.**
The gap is 0.06pt and the CIs overlap almost entirely. The two runs share the
same entries, so the correct test is *paired*, and per-trade nets were not
saved to do it. Honest statement: **both configurations lose significantly;
the difference between them is not established.**

### The finding that matters more than the prediction
Changing only the stop moved the win rate **78.5% → 63.2%** (−15.3pp) and moved
EV by **0.07pt**.

That is the second demonstration in one session:
- vary K → win rate **+16pp**, EV flat negative
- vary the stop → win rate **−15pp**, EV flat negative

EV sits still while the win rate swings ~30 points around it. Optional stopping
shown from two independent directions on 55k trades. **No stop geometry escapes
it** — fixed, scaled, wide, tight, all land in the same place.

### What the watcher must beat (quantified, at K≥5)
`mean_net = P·W − (1−P)·L − 0.89`, with P=0.785, W=5.91, L=20:

| goal | required avg runaway loss | saving needed |
|---|---|---|
| break-even | 17.44pt | **2.56pt** |
| +$2/trade | 12.79pt | 7.21pt |
| +$4/trade | 8.14pt | 11.86pt |

Or via the other lever — hold winners longer: 7.0pt → +$0.62/trade, **8.0pt →
+$2.18/trade**, 9.0pt → +$3.76/trade.

The watcher does not need clairvoyance. It needs ~2.6pt off the losers or ~2pt
more from the winners. **First hard number this program has had for what
judgment must beat.**

### Why the watcher is the only live route
It is the only remaining candidate that is *not a fixed geometry* — the one
thing that can condition on information a rule cannot see. Every fixed rule has

## 12. Split-second tell — SIGNAL REAL (AUC 0.752), RULE LOSES

### Tool: `research/dojo_forge/tools/runaway_early_tell.py`

### Why — the lesson
"Range in the first 20s" is largely *"it is already moving against me, fast"* —

sooner converts recoverable trades into realized losses.

A high AUC that loses money is still a loss — the test was built to catch

## 13. GEOMETRY IS A DEAD END — three tests, 59pp of win rate, identical EV

Owner's live-watcher thread produced three distinct mechanical designs. All were
tested on the same data. The result is the cleanest statement of optional
stopping this program has produced.

| design | win rate | mean net |
|---|---|---|
| moving ±1.5σ band, 20pt stop | **78.5%** | −0.55pt |
| moving ±1.5σ band, 3×σ stop | 63.2% | −0.62pt |
| fixed churn extremes, ping-pong | **19.5%** | −0.50pt |

**The win rate spans 59 percentage points. The EV does not move** — it sits at
≈ −0.5pt in every case, which is gross-EV-zero plus 0.89pt friction, exactly
what the theorem predicts. Proven from three independent directions.

**Conclusion: the entry/exit geometry parameter space contains no edge.** This
is not "we picked bad parameters" — stop searching it.

### §13a Ping-pong between fixed extremes (`pingpong_range_test.py`)
Owner: *"set levels at both extremes and then ping pong on the relative
levels."* His design **fixes a real flaw** in mine — σ bands *move* because the
cubic is a ~zero-lag tracker, so a "traverse" can complete because the mean came
to the price (see §12 note). Fixed price levels cannot chase. He arrived at that
correction independently.

Implemented as specified: levels = high/low of the prior 30min churn window,
taken from bars **strictly before** the touch (else the level is fitted to the
move it predicts); enter at one extreme, target the **opposite** extreme; stop
8pt outside the range; K≥5 regime only.

**N=2,093 over 169 sessions, median range 43pt.** Target hit 19.5%, stopped
72.1%, mean net −0.50pt CI [−1.30, +0.35] (not significant), median −10.14pt.

**Paired against the moving band on identical entries** — the only clean way to
isolate fixed-vs-moving targets:

- fixed extremes −0.50pt · moving band −0.54pt
- **paired Δ +0.03pt, 95% CI [−0.71, +0.83] — not significant**

The correction is right in principle and buys nothing in practice.

### What survives
The one lever that is **not** geometry: **exit timing on winners** (§12
addendum). 10,287 completing traverses leave **6.57pt/winner** unclaimed (MFE
12.52 vs 5.95 captured, median gap 4.00pt). Capturing 25% of it → +0.77pt
(+$1.54); 50% → +2.06pt; perfect → +4.63pt. It requires only *averaging* better
than hold-to-band, not calling tops — and it is precisely what a rule cannot
express and judgment might.

**That is the whole remaining case for the watcher, and it is now numerically
specified.**

## 15. Replication attempt — the edge was a phantom fill

Owner corrected the assignment: *"We do agree that the instruction was solve how

### The hypothesis
The distinguishing feature of his trade was never the entry (the same ±1.5σ
extreme touch tested a dozen times). **It was that he held**, exiting on a
ratchet that retains X% of *peak open profit* — which arms only once profit
exists and never gives back more than a fixed share of the best shown. That
exact combination had never been tested; the old ratchet work trailed from

### Honest fills — the edge is gone

## 16. Exit geometry — exhausted and closed

The owner's "redcon": *"we should have registered the MFE and then hold until it
retraces for 80% and since we already saw the rest we will exit at 70% of that
MFE."* A **two-stage** exit — freeze the reference at the warning, exit at a
lower fraction of the *frozen* value, with the 80/70 gap as hysteresis. That is
structurally different from a continuous ratchet, which chases new highs forever
and exits on the first touch.

Tested on the full corpus across 3.0–5.0σ, four (warn, exit) pairs, against the
single-stage ratchet on identical entries. Release-on-new-high implemented
(a fresh extreme un-freezes), else a recovered trade would exit on a stale
reference.

**Every cell negative.** Best across the entire sweep: 4.5σ, 80%/60% →
**−0.03pt/trade**, 1.8 trades/session, CI **[−1.32, +1.38]** — break-even at
best with enormous uncertainty. Two-stage is not systematically better or worse
than one-stage; at 3.0σ it wins slightly, at 5.0σ it loses badly.

100 sessions had shown a monotone gradient ending at **+0.60** at 4.0σ. The full
603-session run: **−0.47**. The apparent edge was noise. **Correction issued:**
I had told the owner a monotone gradient across five levels was stronger
evidence than any single cell — it was still thin cells, and it did not survive.
Overstated.
