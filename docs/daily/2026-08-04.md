## 2. Bayesian table actuary — built, audited, and then largely DEMOLISHED

Counted Beta posteriors (global rate as a 20-count shrinkage prior),
ACTIONABLE only on surviving day-clustered bootstrap + Benjamini-Hochberg.
`research/bayes_tables/`, lookup layer `tools/actuary.py`, wired into every
dojo freeze beside the calibrated gauge.

**Three bugs I caught myself** before believing anything: wrong outcome
column (unbounded exceed_ref sits at 0.95 and asks nothing); depth buckets
capped at 2pt that silently dropped EVERY breakout row (51% coverage — every
"strongest cell" was a RETURN by construction); pooled lookups unioning
intervals into a useless [41%,100%].

**Then an adversarial audit** (`reports/audit_v0.md`) recomputed all six
tables from raw and found more: the day-clustered CI brackets `raw` while I
reported `post` (7 cells had post OUTSIDE its own interval); PRIOR_STRENGTH
was **decorative** (actionable counts identical at 5/20/50/200 — tested on
one number, reported as another); the backoff dropped a fixed favourite
dimension rather than the one that caused the miss, mispricing my own
headline cell; and **my "direction carries nothing" null was FALSE** —
position-in-box predicts chop escape at AUC 0.620.

Fixed: CI reported next to the unit it was computed on, MIN_DAYS=25 floor
(kills zero-width-CI cells that walked through BH), dims rebuilt on the
audit's dominant variables, culprit-driven backoff, pooled day count.

## 3. THE RESULT THAT MATTERS — it is all first-passage geometry

Rebuilt on the right variables the tables sharpened hugely: chop escape
0.36->0.66, stall new-extreme 39%->0.4%, leg_descent new-low 80%->49%. So I
asked whether any of it is prediction.

**Test 1 — driftless-walk barrier null.** P(hit upper first) =
d_down/(d_up+d_down) from the box edges: observed P(up) 0.5085 vs geometric
null 0.5094, **mean excess -0.0009, day-clustered 95% CI [-0.0078, +0.0063]**.
The entire AUC-0.620 "edge" is the distance ratio.

**Test 2 — symmetric races** (+-10pt, geometry cancels by construction):
stall give_frac quintiles 0.4885-0.5085 (one cell separates by 1.2pt — chance
at 1-in-5), leg_descent defense_pt quintiles 0.4887-0.5047, none separate.
Globals 0.5002 and 0.4962 with tight intervals.

**Conclusion**: the tables encode distance to a barrier, not forecasting. The
0.57 wall stands for the eighth independent time, and this is the cleanest
demonstration yet because the geometry-free version of the SAME events lands
on 50.0%. The tables remain valuable as a RISK instrument ("given where price
sits between my levels, what do I hit first?") — the same verdict the program
reached for the owner's exit protocol and the +-N bracket study. A student
trained on these cells would learn a closed-form distance ratio.

## 4. What this means for the Mamba question

Train it as an **arming model**, not a trading model: onset prediction (0.77)
is real and buys latency for the 200ms fast lane, but the events it predicts
resolve by geometry, so knowing one is coming does not tell you which way it
goes. Building an alpha engine on the tables would be building a very
expensive ruler.

## 2. Verdicts
Replication exact (max abs error 0.0 on every row of all 7 cells).
- **CLEAN**: sign conventions (no `stop_width_study`-class inversion); the
  49% is horizon-invariant (30s..600s); sample overlap is severe (96% of 300s
  windows overlap the previous trigger's) but day-clustered VIF ~= 1.
- **FLAW**: time axis not enforced (`c[i]-c[i-T]` is T BARS not T seconds;
  38% of "60s impulses" took >60s, max 1,346s); 1.4% of follow windows
  truncated at the RTH mask end (avoidable — data runs to 20:00 ET);
  ties counted as losses so the null is 49.79%, not 50%; MFE computed then
  dropped from the report (MFE ~= MAE); "seven independent parameterisations"
  is false (cells are 83-100% nested).
- The report violates 4 mandatory `CLAUDE.md` rules: no 95% CI, no significance
  statement, no PF-based Trade WR, no friction.

## 3. The numbers that matter
- Headline conclusion SURVIVES and strengthens: chase expectancy net of 0.89pt
  friction = **-1.197pt/trigger [-1.700, -0.699]** (D10/T60), significant in all
  7 cells. Gross mean run is NOT significant in 6 of 7. Fading also loses.
- Extended past the grid (it stops at D=20, the owner trades 168pt): worse with
  size — D60/T60 mean run -4.43pt, net -5.32pt.
- "Enormous heat 17.75pt" is a 300s-hold statistic. Random-time random-direction
  control = 13.50pt. Impulse conditioning lifts heat 1.28x, not 8x.
- 62% of the reported heat is created by entering at `t`: p50 MAE 6.50pt at the
  leg start vs 17.25pt at the trigger. But the start anchor is 100% hindsight —
  mean |disp| 15.303 vs mean start-anchor run 15.056 => post-trigger residue
  **-0.246pt**. Nothing to trade there.
- `P(|disp|>=10)` at a RANDOM 60s window = 32.1%. The D10 cells are a third of
  the tape, 61% cooldown saturation. Not an "impulse".

## 4. The owner's claim, tested for the first time
Pre-state at `t-T` vs time-of-day-matched non-impulse controls (D40/T60, the
clean cell — only 18.9% of RTH excluded; at D10/T60 it is 94.3%, so that cell's
AUCs are circular):
- **Timing IS predictable**: realised vol 60s AUC **0.937 [0.907, 0.956]**,
  range 300s 0.915, volume 0.800. Mechanism is volatility clustering.
- **Range COMPRESSION does not precede impulses** (AUC 0.519) — coiling is dead.
- **Direction is NOT**: best pre-feature is position-in-900s-range at 0.585 AUC
  (mean-reverting sign). Everything else 0.50-0.54.
- **"MAE 0" is a 1-in-40 draw**: even entering at the exact leg start with
  perfect hindsight, `P(MAE <= 0.25pt)` over 300s is 2.5-5.6% and p50 MAE is
  6.5-12.25pt. The 2024_09_16 anecdote is also outside the study's data range.

## 5. What to look for next
The trap: the state that predicts an impulse is high volatility, which is the
state of maximum heat. Anticipating timing without solving direction converts an
unbiased coin at high heat into an unbiased coin at high heat entered earlier.
Any follow-up must move direction AUC off ~0.585, not re-measure onset.
