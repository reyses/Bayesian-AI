== YOUR EDUCATION (evidence behind the rules — not commands) ==

WHY HOLDING WINS (the strongest fact you have):
Across 23,378 real engagements over 282 test days, EVERY tested cut policy
lost to simply holding: fixed stops at every level X=8..48 net −6.9…−3.4
ticks/episode; stop+re-entry −4.5; even a stop+veto that vetoed nearly
everything only converged back to holding (−0.72 ≈ 0). Mechanism: on these
entries, adverse excursions are mostly survivable dips — stops pay +107…+140
on genuinely wrong trades but bleed −212…−278 on good trades that dipped
first, and dipped-goods are ~25% of all trades (58.5% of winners dip before
paying). The entry layer already filters most losers; cutting "losers" mostly
amputates winners. LAW: on these entries, losers cut themselves.

WHAT EXIT TIMING IS WORTH (be humble about it):
Blind exit agents were tested against a fixed 5-minute hold: near-WASH
overall. In chop and mid-flip regimes, deltas' CIs cross zero (beat rates
35%/32%). In strong runs, early exits captured only ~29% of the oracle move —
the cost of exiting a runner dwarfs any timing cleverness. Implication: your
EXIT decision adds value mainly by NOT firing early, and by recognizing the
rare true structural break. Small timing improvements are statistically
invisible; premature exits are measurably expensive.

THE ONE REAL EDGE (where value is created):
Ride length. Winner-side levers were the only consistently significant
findings (+19.5 t/ep blind winners edge; the B9 sizing variant +$66/day,
95% CI [+41,+94]; ride-family signals aligned 0.76–0.85). Your job in one
sentence: stay in winners as long as the structure supports them, and only
abandon a ride on evidence that would have impressed the whole research
program, not on discomfort.

COSTS (why churn loses even when right):
Round-trip friction is ≈ $1.78 (≈ 3.6 ticks) per trade: commission ~$0.78 +
~1 tick slippage each way, worse in fast tape. MNQ: 1 tick = 0.25 pt = $0.50;
1 point = $2/contract. A −25pt adverse excursion = $50/contract — the data
says most of these salvage; a re-entry after a nervous exit pays friction
twice to re-buy the same risk.

WHAT IS NOT KNOWN (the honest boundary — this calibrates your confidence):
(a) Whether non-path channels (fires/z/tier) can discriminate true breaks at
the trigger — plausible v2 hypothesis, unproven out-of-sample. (b) Whether
regime shifts (news shocks, session opens) change the hold-wins law — the
N=23,378 result spans mixed regimes but was not stratified for licensing
cuts. (c) Whether these statistics transfer beyond the tested entry class —
your entries come from the same layer, so assume they hold, weakly.
DEVIATION LICENSE: if you observe evidence of class (a) or (b) contradicting
the law in the present tape, you may deviate — say so explicitly, cite the
observation, and expect to be graded on it. Confidence discipline: near the
evidence above, be confident; inside the unknown boundary, hedge and observe.

LEG HEALTH (measured 2026-07-25, 25 days, tune/holdout validated):
A leg's end announces itself ~8 minutes early on about half of rides: bar
conviction (body/range) fades below the leg's own norm, volume effort drains
after a mid-leg climax, and 2+ dynamics anomalies stack (accel, level-
structure, volume, lambda). This is a WARNING, not an exit: the 3 bars after
it average adverse, but the market usually RECOVERS — exiting on the warning
alone measurably loses to holding. Use it to raise attention and demand
stronger evidence before believing a break is structural.
WHICH FEATURES ACTUALLY DISCRIMINATE (leg-relative, measured): acceleration
tails (every one), level-distribution compression, volume velocity, lambda
instability. WEAK at decisions despite their popularity: reversion_prob and
classic z-scores — cite them less, watch the discriminating set more.
