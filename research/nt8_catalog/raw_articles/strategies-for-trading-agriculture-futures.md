# Strategies for Trading Agriculture Futures

By
NinjaTrader Team

July 17, 2025

Futures trading in the agricultural sector brings unique opportunities—and challenges—due to the seasonal nature of crops, weather risks, and global demand shifts. Whether you're new to the space or looking to refine your edge, applying structured [agriculture futures](/futures/futures-contracts/agriculture/) trading strategies can help you make more informed decisions.

Two widely used methods include spread trading and seasonal trend analysis. Let's explore how these approaches work and what factors influence price movement in agricultural markets.

## Spread trading and seasonal analysis in agriculture

Agricultural markets present distinctive price behaviors due to their reliance on natural cycles and evolving global demand. Unlike some other asset classes, agricultural commodities often display predictable patterns tied to planting, growing, and harvest periods, as well as regional supply constraints. Because of this, many experienced traders don’t rely solely on directional bets. Instead, they use strategies that reflect the relative  movement and seasonality of these markets.

Two such strategies—spread trading and seasonal trend analysis—are widely used by traders who aim to take a more structured and informed approach to agriculture futures trading strategies.

#### 1. Spread trading: Capitalizing on price differences

Spread trading involves buying one futures contract while selling another related contract simultaneously. In agriculture, this often means trading the same commodity across different delivery months (a calendar spread) or between two related commodities (an intercommodity spread).

For example, a trader might buy December corn futures and sell March corn futures if they expect prices to tighten between those months. These trades can help reduce exposure to large price swings in the overall market while still presenting opportunities for profit.

#### 2. Seasonal trend analysis: Timing trades with cycles

Many agricultural commodities follow seasonal price cycles influenced by planting and harvest periods, weather conditions, and annual consumption patterns. Seasonal trend analysis helps traders identify repeatable patterns in price behavior.

By reviewing historical charts, you might find that soybean futures tend to rise in late spring due to planting season uncertainties. Timing trades around these patterns can support more strategic entry and exit points.

## What impacts the price of agriculture futures?

Understanding what moves agricultural markets is critical to effective trading. Prices are influenced by a mix of historical data, supply-demand fundamentals, and external events.

#### Historical price data

Analyzing long-term price trends can uncover recurring behaviors and provide a baseline for [strategy development](/futures/futures-trading-basics/futures-trading-plan/). Traders often rely on decades of data to identify how commodities like corn, wheat, and soybeans tend to react during certain times of year or under specific conditions.

#### Supply and demand dynamics

Like all markets, agriculture futures respond strongly to changes in supply and demand. A few key examples:

* A drought in a major grain-producing region may lower supply, pushing prices higher.
* Higher global demand for biofuels can increase corn consumption, affecting futures prices.
* Livestock feed needs may boost demand for soybean meal, another agriculture-linked commodity.

#### Geopolitical influences and trade policy

Events such as tariffs, sanctions, or international trade agreements can shift global supply chains quickly. A new export restriction on wheat from a major producer could drive up prices on the global stage.

Staying aware of these developments can help you anticipate potential market shifts—and manage your risk accordingly.
