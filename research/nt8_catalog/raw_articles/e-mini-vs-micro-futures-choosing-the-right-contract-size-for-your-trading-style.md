# E-mini vs. Micro futures: What contract size is right for you?

By
NinjaTrader Team

November 15, 2024

Think of the futures markets like an amusement park. You’ve got big, heart-pounding, full-size standard [futures contracts](/futures/futures-contracts/), along with less intense yet still exhilarating micro-sized contracts. Each of these contracts offers unique experience and application, appealing to traders with different account sizes, risk thresholds, and strategic goals.

Whether you’re a thrill-seeking trader with a robust account looking to leverage larger moves or a cautious newcomer wanting to dip your toes without overcommitting, understanding the differences between E-mini and Micro contracts is essential. In this blog, we’ll break down the specifications of each, explore the types of traders they attract, and highlight the benefits of Micros, especially when it comes to flexibility, risk control, and cost of entry.

## Understanding the Specifications of E-mini and Micro Futures

* **Standard E-mini futures:** Often referred to as the standard-sized contract for most CME Group related Globex markets, standard E-mini futures provide exposure to markets like the [S&P 500](/futures/futures-contracts/equity-index/e-mini-s-p-500/), [Nasdaq 100](/futures/futures-contracts/equity-index/e-mini-nasdaq/), [gold](/futures/futures-contracts/metals/gold/), [crude oil](/futures/futures-contracts/energy/crude-oil/), and others. With these contract sizes, each point move represents a higher dollar value, providing higher potential profit with substantial risk. Typically, these contracts are used by traders with larger account sizes and more experience.
* **Micro futures:** Introduced more recently by CME Group, Micro futures offer access to the same underlying assets as E-minis but in a fraction of the size, generally one tenth (1/10) the size of standard contracts. For instance, while each point move in an E-mini S&P 500 contract represents $50, the same move in a [Micro contract](/futures/futures-contracts/micro-emini-futures/) is just $5. This reduced size opens the door for newer, more moderate traders or those just wanting greater flexibility in managing their positions.

## Who’s Trading What?

* **E-mini traders:** E-minis are favored by seasoned, more knowledgeable traders with larger capital reserves. These traders often have higher risk tolerance and look to maximize gains from larger positions. They may also be swing traders, holding positions for days and weeks, with strategies that can withstand larger trade drawdowns.
* **Micro traders:** Micros appeal to a broader range of traders, particularly those just starting out, those with smaller dollar accounts, or even experienced traders who want to fine-tune control over their positions. The smaller contract size makes Micros ideal for learning, testing strategies, or practicing discipline and risk management without exposing significant capital.

## Advantages of Trading Micro Futures Contracts

* **Position sizing flexibility:** Micro contracts allow traders to adjust their exposure incrementally. Instead of committing to a large single position, traders can build or reduce positions gradually, making it easier to scale in and out of trades. This is invaluable for testing ideas or adjusting to market conditions without a heavy initial outlay.
* **Better risk control:** Smaller contracts mean smaller risk per point move, allowing traders to manage risk more precisely. With Micros, traders can add or remove exposure based on their tolerance levels and changing market sentiment, allowing for a more measured approach to risk management.
* **Lower cost of entry:** Micros provide a low-cost entry into futures trading, with lower margin requirements and minimal upfront commitment. For new traders or those testing strategies, this reduced barrier can be a game-changer, providing the benefits of futures trading with less financial pressure.

## Your Gateway to Futures Trading

Whether you’re diving into futures for the thrill of it or seeking a new strategic trading journey, there’s a contract size that will fit your trading style. Standard E-minis take you right into the big leagues, rewarding traders who are better prepared and well capitalized. Micros, on the other hand, can offer a less stressful experience, perfect for building confidence, gaining skills, and learning how to better manage risk.

No matter which contract you choose, each offers a gateway to futures trading with its own unique benefits. So, buckle up, pick the contract that best matches your trading goals, and enjoy the ride.
