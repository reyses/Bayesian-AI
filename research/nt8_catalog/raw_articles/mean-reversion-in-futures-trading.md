# Mean Reversion in Futures Trading: What It Is and How It Works

By
NinjaTrader Team

May 22, 2025

In the dynamic world of futures trading, staying ahead often means leveraging proven strategies that offer structure and consistency. One such strategy is mean reversion—a concept rooted in the idea that prices tend to move back toward their average over time. If you’re looking to build a more systematic approach to futures trading, understanding mean reversion can give you a valuable edge.

## What is mean reversion in futures trading?

At its core, mean reversion is the belief that asset prices and historical returns eventually revert to their long-term mean or average. In futures trading, this could apply to price levels and statistical indicators such as moving averages and standard deviations.

Imagine a market where a futures contract has a sudden spike or drop in price. A mean reversion trader would see this as an opportunity, anticipating that the price will revert to its average once the short-term momentum fades.

## Why mean reversion matters when trading futures

Futures markets are known for their volatility, creating frequent short-term price deviations where mean reversion strategies thrive. Here's why this approach often appeals to futures traders:

* **Market neutrality:** Mean reversion in futures trading doesn’t rely on long-term trends. Whether prices are rising or falling, this strategy focuses on short-term overextensions.
* **Quantifiable entries and exits:** Traders often use indicators (e.g., Bollinger Bands, moving averages, relative strength index [RSI]) to define entry and exit points with more precision.
* **Risk management:** Because trades are typically short-term, traders can control their risk more tightly.

## Common mean reversion indicators

Traders often use a combination of technical indicators to signal potential reversion opportunities. Here are a few of the most popular ones:

* [**Bollinger Bands**](/futures/blogs/bollinger-bands-explained-a-futures-traders-guide/)**:** These bands expand and contract based on volatility. Price movements outside of the bands often suggest overbought or oversold conditions.
* [**Relative strength index (RSI)**](/futures/blogs/relative-strength-index-rsi-measure-price-movement/)**:** This momentum oscillator can help identify overbought and oversold levels.
* [**Moving averages**](/futures/blogs/identifying-trend-with-moving-averages/) **(simple and exponential):** Comparing price to a moving average can highlight potential reversion setups.

## How mean reversion strategies work

Here’s a five-step process for setting up mean reversion in futures trading:

1. **Identify the mean:** Use a 20-day simple moving average (SMA) as the baseline.
2. **Spot divergence:** Look for prices that move significantly above or below the average.
3. **Confirm with indicators:** Use the RSI or Bollinger Bands to confirm the potential for reversion.
4. **Enter the trade:** Go long when prices are below the average and conditions are oversold; go short when prices are above and overbought.
5. **Set stops and limits:** Manage risk by setting a stop-loss and taking profits as price moves back toward the mean.

## Is mean reversion right for you?

Mean reversion in futures trading appeals to traders who prefer defined rules and shorter trade durations. While it works best in range-bound markets, it can be challenging during strong trends, when prices may not return to the mean for extended periods. But with the right tools, practice, and risk management, this strategy can help you refine your futures trading decisions and reduce your emotional bias.
