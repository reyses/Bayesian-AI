## Examples of Three Macroeconomic Indicators Impacting Futures Trading

* **Interest rates**: Interest rates are pivotal macroeconomic indicators that influence the cost of borrowing money. They directly affect consumer spending, business investment, and overall economic activity. Lower interest rates can stimulate economic growth by making loans cheaper, thus encouraging spending and investment, while higher interest rates can help tame inflation but may also slow economic growth.
* **Gross domestic product (GDP)**: GDP measures the total economic output of the U.S., representing the sum of all goods and services produced quarter to quarter. It's a broad indicator of economic health, reflecting the economy's size and growth rate. An increasing GDP indicates a growing economy, while a declining GDP suggests economic contraction.
* **U****nemployment rate**: The unemployment rate is the percentage of the labor force that is jobless and actively seeking employment. It's a key indicator of economic health; high unemployment rates are often associated with economic decline while low unemployment rates can signal a strong economy.

Macroeconomic indicators can help you approach futures trading holistically. Studying indicators like interest rates and GDP are just a couple of the factors that you can study as you trade futures.
