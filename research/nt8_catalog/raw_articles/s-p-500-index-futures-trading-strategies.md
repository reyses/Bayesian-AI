# S&P 500 Index Futures Trading Strategies

By
NinjaTrader Team

February 07, 2025

Whether you’re new to the futures markets or a seasoned trader, going into a trading session without a strategy is a recipe for failure. Although there’s no magic bullet when it comes to picking your spots to buy and sell, there are a number of techniques successful traders often employ when looking at the markets.

For example, trading [E-mini S&P 500 futures](/futures/futures-contracts/equity-index/e-mini-s-p-500/) calls for the same careful approach as any other trading instrument. With that in mind, here’s a quick overview of a few commonly used S&P 500 futures trading strategies you may be able to fit into your overall trading toolbox.

## 1. Trend Gap Strategy

A trend gap strategy is when traders exploit daily price gaps within the context of a longer trend, where these gaps can signify a potentially strong market move.

Here’s a breakdown of how such a strategy might work: A trend gap occurs when the price “gaps” up or down between the close of one day and the open of the next, with a visual gap on the chart. A trend gap can be classified as “breakaway” or “runaway” and occur at the beginning of a new trend direction or during a strong existing trend and reconfirm the trend.

Trade in the direction of the opening gap with a tight stop-loss or trailing stop order in place. Using tools like moving averages, MACD, and momentum indicators can help ensure the gap direction aligns with the current trend. High volume during the gap event can also indicate stronger conviction behind the price move.

## 2. Pullback Strategy

In futures trading, no market moves in a straight line up or down. In a strong trend, prices often pull back to a support or resistance level before continuing the trend, giving traders an opportunity to enter the market at an improved price.

The 21 simple moving average (SMA) often acts as a dynamic support or resistance level in trending markets, providing a reference point for identifying a pullback. The price action in a trend often hits a new high or low and then pulls back to the 21 SMA for a few bars before resuming its direction. This pullback strategy leverages the natural “noise” in a trend with an opportunity to reenter in the dominant market direction.

Once the price pulls back to the 21 SMA, enter immediately in the direction of the trend and    
use a tight stop-loss based on previous historical pullbacks to give you some guidance. If you want to be a bit more aggressive, scale into a second position once price breaks out to a new high (uptrend) or low (downtrend). With two units, you can take profits on one and let the other unit run to capture a larger move.

## 3. Fade Strategy

You often hear the trading adage, “Don’t try to pick tops and bottoms.” But sometimes, that’s exactly where the opportunity lies. The fade strategy does just that, using a price channel as the guide for buy and sell levels. By targeting tops and bottoms in a consolidating or sideways market, this futures trading strategy provides higher probability trades when markets are not trending.

Markets only trend about 40% of the time, leaving the remaining 60% of the time in consolidation or sideways movement. In these conditions, price tends to oscillate between support and resistance levels, often captured within a price channel. The fade strategy takes advantage of this behavior by entering short trades at the upper boundary of the channel and long trades at the lower boundary.

While counterintuitive to newer traders, a fade strategy can achieve a win rate of 55-65% as markets consolidate, making it an effective tool if you properly manage your profit target and stop levels (risk-reward ratio). With a 60% win rate and a 50-50 risk-reward ratio, you create a strategy with a potentially positive long-term return on investment. Look to combine overbought and oversold indicators like Stochastic, Williams %R, and relative strength index (RSI) to help better time entry points.
