# Getting Started Trading Futures: Understanding Futures Contracts, Part 1

By
NinjaTrader Team

December 26, 2025

Futures trading can open the door to a wide range of market opportunities—whether you’re interested in major market equity indices, commodities, or currencies. If you're new to the world of futures or simply exploring how to trade futures, understanding the fundamentals of futures contracts is the first step.

Let’s break down what futures contracts are, how a futures transaction works, the types of orders you can place, and the most popular markets traders watch.

## What are futures contracts?

At its core, a futures contract is a standardized agreement to buy or sell an underlying asset at a predetermined price on a specific date in the future. These contracts are traded on regulated exchanges and cover a wide range of markets, from energy and metals to agricultural products and financial indices.

Unlike traditional investing, where you may buy and hold a security for the long term, futures contracts are typically used for shorter-term strategies. Because they are leveraged products, they allow traders to control larger positions with less capital, enabling flexibility for both speculation and risk management.

Futures contracts come in different sizes, including Micro contracts that make it easier for newer traders to get started with smaller commitments.

[Learn more about the foundations of futures trading.](/futures/futures-trading-basics/)

## What is a futures transaction?

A futures transaction occurs when two parties agree to the terms of a contract: one agrees to buy (go long), and the other agrees to sell (go short) the underlying asset at a later date. These trades are facilitated by futures exchanges and cleared through a centralized clearinghouse, which helps ensure that both sides of the contract are honored.

While each contract has a set expiration date, most retail traders don’t hold positions until the end. Instead, they close their trades before expiration, offsetting their position with an opposite order. This approach allows them to focus on price changes, not physical delivery.

For example, if a trader buys a gold futures contract expecting the price to rise, they can sell the contract later if the market moves in their favor, capturing any gains (or losses) based on price movement.

## Types of futures orders

As you learn to trade, it’s important to understand the different order types used to enter and exit positions. Each order type helps you decide how and when your trade is executed:

* **Market order:** Executes the entry or exit trade immediately at the best available price.
* **Limit order:**Executes only at a specific price or better, helping you manage entry and exit levels; often used to set a profit target.
* **Stop-loss order:** Closes a position once the market reaches a predefined price; often used to help manage downside risk.
* **One cancels the other (OCO) order:** Links a stop-loss and take-profit order so that triggering one cancels the other automatically.

These tools can be valuable when trading fast-moving futures markets, helping you stay focused and disciplined in your approach.

[Learn more about how to place a futures order.](/learn/how-to-trade-futures/place-a-futures-order/)

## Popular futures markets

Futures contracts are available across a wide range of global markets. Here are some of the most commonly traded categories:

* **Equity index futures:** Trade the overall performance of stock indices like the S&P 500 (ES), Nasdaq 100 (NQ), and Dow Jones Industrial Average (YM) via E-mini and Micro E-mini contracts.
* **Energy futures:** Includes contracts such as crude oil (CL) and natural gas (NG), which are known for high liquidity and volatility.
* **Metal futures:** Popular markets like gold (GC), silver (SI), and copper (HG) offer exposure to both precious and industrial metals.
* **Agricultural futures:** Markets for corn (ZC), soybeans (ZS), and wheat (ZW) let traders engage with global food supply and demand trends.
* **Currency futures:** Also called forex futures, these include contracts like the euro, British pound, and Japanese yen against the U.S. dollar.
* **Bitcoin futures:** These let you participate in the dynamic cryptocurrencies markets and come in smaller Micro contracts.

Each market has its own unique characteristics and specifications—such as contract size, trading hours, expiration schedules, delivery methods, volatility, and margin requirements—so it’s important to explore them all and find what fits your trading goals and risk tolerance best.
