# Why the CPI Report and Other Economic Indicators Affect Futures

By
NinjaTrader Team

July 23, 2024

Inflation is one of the crucial economic indicators that greatly impacts market sentiment for many key futures markets including bonds, currencies, and major market stock indices. The two primary metrics used to measure inflation are the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and the Producer Price Index (PPI). Both reports provide valuable insights for traders related to current and trending inflation data.

## What is Inflation?

You experience inflation when the prices of things you buy regularly like food, gasoline, and clothes go up over time. It means that the amount of money you’re spending today buys less than it did before. Consumer retail inflation is calculated and published in the monthly [CPI report](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, which is a part of the U.S. Department of Labor.

### What does the CPI measure?

The CPI measures the seasonally adjusted average change in prices paid by consumers for goods and services over time. It’s a critical indicator of inflation at the retail level and is widely used by economists, FOMC policymakers, and futures traders.

**CPI components:**

* Food and beverages: Prices of groceries and dining out
* Housing: Rent, utility bills, and home prices
* Apparel: Clothing and footwear
* Transportation: Vehicle prices, gasoline, and public transport
* Medical care: Healthcare services and prescription drugs
* Recreation: Entertainment and recreational services
* Education and communication: Tuition fees, telephone services, and internet

### Impact of Inflation on Futures Trading

With recent CPI reports indicating lower inflation, this may also signal a lowering of interest rates. For futures traders, lower inflation can often lead to increased market stability and lower [volatility](/learn/technical-analysis/use-volatility-as-indicator-in-futures-trading/). Both bond and note futures may see an increase in bullish sentiment as lower interest rates can boost bond prices and reduce yields. Additionally, lower bond yields can weaken the U.S. dollar (USD), which has the potential to increase the price of those commodities transacted in USD.

### What is PPI and How is it Different from CPI?

The PPI measures the average change in selling prices received by domestic producers for their output. Unlike CPI, which focuses on the retail level, PPI captures price changes at the wholesale level, providing an early indication of inflationary trends.

**PPI components:**

* Finished goods: Prices of goods ready for sale
* Intermediate goods: Prices of goods that require further processing
* Crude materials: Prices of raw materials

PPI data is also valuable for commodity futures traders, as changes in production costs can affect the supply and demand dynamics of commodities like energy, metals, and agricultural products.

Understanding the CPI and PPI is crucial for interpreting inflation trends to help better anticipate short- and long-term market sentiment and trends. As economic indicators, both CPI and PPI provide valuable insights into the health of the U.S. economy and the potential direction of future prices, making them essential tools for any active futures trader.
