# Interest Rate Policy and Futures Trading: Fed Walks Tightrope on Decision

By
NinjaTrader Team

August 01, 2024

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is the monetary policymaking body of the Federal Reserve System (aka the Fed) in the U.S. One of the FOMC's primary responsibilities is setting the federal funds target rate, which is the interest rate at which depository institutions lend balances to each other overnight. By adjusting this target rate, the FOMC can influence the overall level of interest rates in the economy.

The Fed faces a delicate balancing act as it navigates the current economic landscape. In a recent [NinjaTrader Live](/learn/livestreams/) segment, renowned economist Blu Putnam discussed the Fed's cautious approach to potential interest rate cuts, the market's expectations, and the economic indicators the central bank is monitoring.

**Key Takeaways**

* **Jobless claims and economic indicators:**We analyzed the latest jobless claims data, noting the seasonally adjusted numbers ticking higher and the non-seasonally adjusted numbers ticking lower.It’s important to monitor the pace of change in jobless claims, which is seeing the four-week moving average creeping higher—a key metric for a possible rate cut in September.
* **Fed Chair Powell's press conference:**Chairman Powell's handling of questions in his post-FOMC press conference were primarily focused on his desire to start cutting interest rates but also on the need to wait for more economic data before making a final decision on a September cut.
* **Rate cut expectations and market sentiment:**The market has shifted its expectations, now pricing in a 100% chance of a rate cut at the next FOMC meeting and a 94% chance of all three possible remaining cuts by the end of 2024.
* **Yield curve:** An inverted yield curve has led to a recession seven out of seven times in the past. When the next recession will occur, how long it will last, and how severe is anyone's guess. The good news is that the Fed has plenty of room to move on interest rates to mitigate almost any economic scenario.

## Importance of the Federal Funds Rate for Futures Traders

Futures traders closely monitor FOMC meetings and statements to gauge the direction of interest rates and adjust their positions accordingly.

When the FOMC lowers interest rates, it can stimulate economic growth by making borrowing more affordable and encouraging spending and investment. When the FOMC raises interest rates, it typically leads to higher borrowing costs for businesses and consumers, which can slow down economic growth by discouraging spending and investment.

Interest rate policy is highly relevant to futures traders, as changes in interest rates can have a significant impact on various futures markets. For example, higher interest rates can strengthen the U.S. dollar, which can put downward pressure on commodity prices denominated in dollars, such as [gold](/futures/futures-contracts/metals/gold/) and [oil futures](/futures/futures-contracts/energy/crude-oil/).

Additionally, interest rate changes can affect [bond futures](/futures/futures-contracts/us-treasury-bonds/), with higher rates typically leading to lower bond prices and vice versa. [Equity index futures](/futures/futures-contracts/equity-index/), such as those based on the [S&P 500](/futures/futures-contracts/equity-index/e-mini-s-p-500/), can also be influenced by interest rate policy, as higher rates can make fixed-income investments more attractive relative to [stocks](/futures/why-trade-futures/futures-vs-stocks/), potentially leading to a shift in asset allocation.

The Fed faces a challenging task in navigating the current economic environment, balancing its desire to support the economy with the need to maintain price and employment stability. The complexity surrounding the central bank's decision-making process and the importance of closely monitoring a wide range of economic indicators makes their role and mission ever more challenging.

All futures traders should keep a close eye on the Fed’s upcoming decisions to help them make smarter, more informed trading decisions.
