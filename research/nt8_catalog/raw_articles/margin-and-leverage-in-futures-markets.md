# Why margin and leverage are key to trading futures

By
NinjaTrader Team

March 12, 2025

When trading futures, understanding the concepts of margin and leverage is essential. These two financial tools give traders the ability to control large positions with a relatively small amount of capital.\* However, they also come with increased risk, so it’s important for traders to know how to manage these aspects effectively. In this post, we’ll explore what margin and leverage mean in the futures markets, how they work together, and how traders can use them to their advantage.

## **What Is Margin In Futures Trading?**

In futures trading, your trading account is a margin account. This is very different from a traditional margin account used in stock trading, where investors borrow funds and incur interest charges. In the futures markets, margin serves as a good-faith deposit, enabling traders to open and hold positions without borrowing funds.

For more detailed information, read [*Using Margin in Futures Trading*](/learn/how-to-trade-futures/using-margin/)*.*

## **Types Of Margins In Futures Trading**

1. **Initial margin:** This is the upfront deposit required to open a position in the futures markets. This amount is set by the futures exchange and your broker and varies depending on the contract’s volatility and risk profile.

2. **Maintenance margin:** Once a position is open, the maintenance margin is the minimum equity a trader must maintain in their account. If the account balance falls below this level due to market fluctuations, a margin call is triggered, requiring the trader to add funds to maintain the position.

## **Day Trading Margin**

Margin requirements in futures trading can be significantly lower during day trading hours. For example, NinjaTrader offers day trading margins on popular contracts for as low as $50, allowing traders to enter positions with even less capital. Margin requirements revert back to the normal overnight maintenance margin once the trading session closes.

## **What Is Leverage In Futures Trading****?**

Futures trading leverage allows traders to control a much larger contract value with a smaller margin deposit, giving them power to magnify both gains and losses. Calculating leverage is straightforward: Divide the notional value (the total value of the underlying asset in the contract) by the required margin.

Keep in mind that leverage can vary depending on trading conditions and margin levels. For example, if crude oil is trading at $70 a barrel, where one crude oil futures contract represents 1,000 barrels, the notional value of the contract would be $70,000. If the initial margin requirement is $7,000, the leverage ratio would be 10:1 ($70,000 ÷ $7,000). A lower margin requirement, such as $1,000 for day trading, would create a higher leverage ratio of 70:1.

Using leverage in futures trading offers potential for significant profits, but it also increases risk. Traders must employ [risk management](/futures/futures-trading-basics/risk-management/) strategies to help mitigate losses in leveraged trades, such as placing stop-loss or trailing stop orders.

## **Understanding The Relationship Between Margin And Leverage**

Margin and leverage are interdependent in futures trading. While margin sets the minimum amount needed to enter a position, leverage determines the size of the position that can be controlled with that margin. In practice, margin and leverage allow traders to allocate capital efficiently across multiple positions or trades. However, with this increased potential for gains also comes the increased risk of significant losses.

Trading with leverage means that even small market movements can have substantial impacts on the overall profit or loss of a position. This is why effective risk management is essential for any trader using margin and leverage.

## **How Much Leverage Is In Futures****Trading?**

The amount of leverage available in [futures trading](/futures/futures-contracts/) varies by market and can be quite high. Some futures contracts offer leverage ratios of up to 100:1, meaning a trader could control a contract worth $100,000 with just $1,000 in margin.

While high leverage can be attractive, it’s important to remember that it also increases risk exposure. With higher leverage, even a small adverse move in the market could result in a margin call or the liquidation of a position if adequate funds are not maintained. It’s important to keep in mind that it is possible to lose more money than is in your account.

## **Pros And Cons Of Futures Trading With Leverage**

Trading futures with leverage offers unique benefits and potential drawbacks:

#### **Pros** **of Futures Trading with Leverage**

1. **Lower capital requirement:** Leverage enables traders to enter positions with less capital, freeing up resources for other trades and investments.
2. **Potential for higher returns:** When positions are highly leveraged, small movements in the market can result in significant profits.

#### **Cons** **of Futures Trading with Leverage**

1. **Increased risk of losses:** Just as leverage amplifies gains, it also amplifies losses. Even a small unfavorable price movement can lead to a large percentage loss.
2. **Margin calls:** When market conditions are volatile, margin calls may be frequent, requiring additional funds to keep positions open.

Traders can better manage leverage by implementing strategies such as risk assessments and using stop-loss orders to limit potential losses.

## **Strategies For Using Margin And Leverage Effectively**

Futures traders often follow these best practices to maximize the potential of margin and leverage:

1. **Use risk capital only:** Due to the risks associated with high leverage, only risk capital (funds a trader can afford to lose) should be used in futures trading.

2. **Set realistic expectations:** While leverage can boost profits, it also means that losses are proportionally larger. Setting realistic profit targets and being prepared for potential losses can help traders stay grounded.

3. **Practice risk management**: Placing stop-loss and trailing stop orders can help minimize losses if the market moves unfavorably. Additionally, diversifying positions across different markets—such as [precious metal futures](/futures/futures-contracts/metals/), [energy futur](/futures/futures-contracts/energy/)es, and [index futures](/futures/futures-contracts/index/)—can reduce overall risk.

4. **Stay informed:** Market conditions and [margin requirements](/pricing/margins/) can change rapidly. Staying informed about contract specifications and margin adjustments is crucial for success.

## **Making the Most of Leverage and Margin**

In futures trading, margin and leverage are powerful tools that can help traders maximize returns with relatively small capital. However, these tools require a thorough understanding of the risks involved. By practicing risk management and staying informed about market conditions, traders can make the most of leverage and margin to optimize their trading strategies.
