# Market Correlation and Intramarket Relationships in Futures

By
NinjaTrader Team

June 17, 2024

Understanding the concept of market correlation is essential for recognizing relationships between seemingly similar and unrelated futures markets. Correlation refers to the statistical relationship between two or more markets, indicating how prices move in relation to each other over time.

A positive correlation indicates that the prices of two markets tend to move in the same direction, and a negative (inverse) correlation suggests that the prices move in opposite directions. There can also be a lack of correlation or zero correlation, implying that the price changes between two markets are mostly unrelated. Learning how and why correlations are formed can help traders make better, more informed trading decisions.

In a recent episode of NinjaTrader Live, Anthony Crudele discussed how correlations, whether positive or negative, are prevalent throughout the futures markets. He also explained how to identify these correlations and use them in your daily trading regimen.

## The Pitfalls of Market Correlation

As market conditions change, previously correlated assets can diverge from established patterns. For example, economic events, geopolitical developments, and changes in supply and demand can change market sentiment and disrupt established correlations. Traders need to stay on top of changing conditions so they’re not caught off guard when these relationships are in flux.

Additionally, correlation does not imply causation. Just because two assets move together does not mean there is a direct causal relationship between them, nor that they will continue to move together in the future. Overreliance on correlation without considering the underlying factors and reasons behind these price relationships can lead to inconsistent trading results.

### How Market Correlation is Used in Trading

Traders often look at correlated markets to confirm trade signals. If [crude oil](/futures/futures-contracts/energy/crude-oil/) and gasoline futures exhibit a positive correlation, a rise in oil prices might predict a similar rise in gasoline prices, providing a stronger basis for entering a trade. Similarly, oppositely correlated markets can offer hedging opportunities. For example, bonds and the U.S. dollar often show a negative correlation, whereas a rise in bonds might correspond with a fall in U.S. dollar prices.

Understanding correlations in the futures markets can be a powerful analysis tool for traders. By identifying and analyzing the relationships between different markets, traders can make better, more informed decisions, enhance their [risk management](/futures/futures-trading-basics/risk-management/) process, and improve the overall consistency of their [trading plan](/futures/futures-trading-basics/futures-trading-plan/).
