# What Happens When a Futures Contract Expires?

By
NinjaTrader Team

November 17, 2025

Futures contracts have a set expiration date that marks the end of their trading period. Understanding what happens when a futures contract expires can help traders avoid unexpected outcomes.

Let’s examine how expiration and settlement work, plus what steps you can take before expiration to manage your positions.

## Understanding futures contract expiration

A futures contract is a legally binding agreement to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a specific future date. That specific date is the contract’s expiration date: generally, it’s the last trading day, when the contract is settled. However, some futures have a last trading date prior to expiration date, so always check before trading.

For example, crude oil and some agricultural commodities may expire monthly, while equity index futures always follow a quarterly schedule. As expiration nears, contracts typically see increased volume and volatility, especially as traders adjust or close their positions.

## Physical vs. cash settlements

When a futures contract expires, it’s settled in one of two ways: cash settlement or physical settlement (delivery).

* **Cash settlement:** Cash-settled futures contracts don’t involve delivery of a physical asset, like gold or crude oil. Instead, they are settled based on the final price of the underlying asset at expiration. Any gains or losses are reflected directly in the trader’s account balance. Cash settlement is typical for financial instruments like equity index futures and volatility products.
* **Physical settlement:** Physically settled futures contracts involve the actual delivery of the underlying asset, such as barrels of crude oil or bushels of corn. When trading physically settled futures, it’s important to know that if you don’t intend to take delivery, the trading day prior to first notice date (FND) is your last trading date. In practice: Plan your closing trade or roll ahead of time, set reminders, and monitor daily delivery notices to avoid auto- liquidation by your broker.

It's important to note that most traders close their position prior to the expiration date to avoid any settlement.

## What traders can do before a contract expires

As contract expiration or FND approaches, traders have a few options for managing their open positions.

* **Close the position:** Most traders choose to close their positions before the FND or the contract expires. This involves executing an offsetting trade to exit the market, either locking in profits or limiting losses.
* **Roll the position forward:** Traders who want to maintain exposure to a market can “roll” their position. This involves closing the existing contract and opening a new one in the next active month.

Choosing the right approach depends on your goals, risk tolerance, and how the specific contract is settled at expiration.

## How expiration affects pricing and liquidity

As expiration or FND approaches, trading volume tends to shift from the front-month (expiring) contract to the next month’s contract. This shift can impact both pricing and execution:

* **Reduced liquidity:** The affected contract may see thinner order books and wider bid-ask spreads, increasing the cost of entering or exiting a position.
* **Temporary pricing differences:**These can occur between contract months due to market expectations. Common scenarios include:
  + **Contango:**Future prices are higher than the spot price.
  + **Backwardation:** Future prices are lower than the spot price.

To avoid these inefficiencies, many traders transition to the next contract during the rollover period, when volume begins to favor the newer contract.

## Managing risk during expiration periods

Contract expiration or FND can introduce operational and market risk if not handled carefully. Here are a few ways to help stay on top of your positions:

* **Monitor expiration, FND, and rollover dates:**Keep a calendar or use platform tools to stay informed.
* **Avoid last-minute trades:** Liquidity can drop off quickly, leading to slippage or difficulty executing orders.
* **Adjust margin and position sizing:** As volatility picks up near expiration, ensure your positions align with your risk tolerance.
* **Use stop-loss and take-profit orders:** These can help manage risk if price movements accelerate near expiration.
