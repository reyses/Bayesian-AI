# How to Blend Technical and Fundamental Analysis in Futures Trading

By
NinjaTrader Team

May 19, 2025

In futures trading, traders use various tools and strategies to interpret market behavior and evaluate potential trade opportunities. Two of the most widely used methods are [technical analysis](/learn/technical-analysis-made-easy/) and fundamental analysis. While each approach can offer its own unique insights, many traders explore ways to combine the two to form a more well-rounded view of the markets.

## What is technical analysis in futures trading?

Technical analysis involves studying historical price charts and trading volume to [identify patterns](/learn/technical-analysis/identify-chart-patterns-on-futures-trading-charts/) and [potential market trends](/learn/technical-analysis/identifying-trends-in-futures-markets/). Futures traders use tools like moving averages, oscillators, and chart patterns to help guide their decisions, particularly in shorter time frames.

#### Common technical indicators used by futures traders

Technical indicators like these can help you analyze momentum, identify potential turning points, and establish areas of interest based on price behavior:

* Moving averages
* Relative strength index (RSI)
* Moving average convergence divergence (MACD)
* Support and resistance levels
* Candlestick patterns

## What is fundamental analysis?

Fundamental analysis focuses on the underlying factors that influence supply and demand in a market. For futures traders, this can include:

* Weather patterns for agricultural commodities
* Inventory reports for energy markets
* Central bank policy announcements
* Macroeconomic indicators like inflation or employment data

This type of analysis can offer a broader context for market activity and is often used to assess long-term trends and the potential impact of economic events on prices.

## Why combine both approaches?

Technical and fundamental analysis in futures trading serve different purposes. Technical analysis can help with trade timing and [risk management](/futures/futures-trading-basics/risk-management/), while fundamental analysis can provide insight into broader market direction or sentiment.

Some traders choose to blend these methods to:

* **Cross-reference perspectives:** Use fundamentals to support or question what technical indicators are suggesting
* **Clarify market context:** Understand whether technical setups align with broader economic trends or data
* **Develop balanced trade ideas:** Combine price-based signals with economic insights to build a more comprehensive trading framework

**Example: Analyzing crude oil futures**

Consider a scenario where [crude oil](/futures/futures-contracts/energy/crude-oil/) prices are forming a technical breakout pattern. You might look at the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) weekly inventory report to understand current supply conditions. If inventory levels are falling, this data may reinforce the technical pattern you’re observing and help you make a more informed decision.

## Best practices for a blended approach

If you’re interested in combining technical and fundamental analysis in futures trading, start with these four best practices:

1. **Follow relevant market news:** Identify the key reports or data releases that influence the futures contracts you’re trading.
2. **Use technical tools to structure trade ideas:** Apply indicators to identify trends, support/resistance levels, and entry and exit points.
3. **Keep a trading journal:** Track how different types of analysis influence your decision-making process over time.
4. **Practice in a simulated environment:** Experiment with different combinations to help you develop a personalized trading approach.

## Put your blend to the test

There’s no single “correct” way to analyze the futures markets. Both technical and fundamental analysis in futures trading offer different perspectives that can contribute to your overall approach. Blending these methods may give you a more structured way to evaluate trading opportunities and manage risk.

To find the strategies that work best for your goals, try integrating both technical and fundamental viewpoints into your trading routine.
