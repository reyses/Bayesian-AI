# 5 Ways to Avoid Exchange Rule Violations in Futures Trading

By
NinjaTrader Team

May 29, 2025

When you're actively trading futures, staying compliant with exchange rules isn't just a good practice—it’s essential. Violating these rules can lead to penalties, fines, or even suspension from trading, putting both your strategy and capital at risk. But the good news? With a few smart habits, you can trade with confidence and avoid unnecessary hiccups.

Here’s a general overview of what you need to know to stay in bounds.

## What are exchange rules and why do they matter?

Every futures exchange—like CME Group or the ICE—has a set of rules that governs how trading must be conducted on its platform, designed to ensure fair access, market integrity, and financial safety for all participants.

Whether you're trading E-mini futures or exploring agricultural contracts, you’re expected to follow the same rulebook as institutional traders. That means keeping pace with everything from position limits to order types and margin requirements.

## Common rule violations (and how to avoid them)

Let’s look at some of the most common pitfalls that can lead to exchange rule violations—and how you can sidestep them.

#### 1. Exceeding position limits

Each futures contract has a maximum number of positions a trader can hold. These limits help prevent market manipulation and protect against extreme volatility.

**Avoid it:** Always check position limits for each product you trade. Your futures broker or trading platform should help you monitor your exposure.

#### 2. Improper use of order types

Some traders use prohibited or unsupported order types during specific market conditions, often unknowingly.

**Avoid it:** Familiarize yourself with which order types are allowed on each exchange—especially during volatile or illiquid periods.

#### 3. Failure to meet margin requirements

Margins are your minimum capital commitment to hold a position. Falling short. even temporarily, can result in liquidation or margin calls.

**Avoid it:** Use real-time margin monitoring tools, and make sure your account is always adequately funded, particularly in fast-moving markets.

#### 4. Spoofing and layering

These are illegal practices where traders place orders they intend to cancel in an effort to influence prices.

**Avoid it:** Stick to your genuine trading intentions. Don't use orders to send false signals to the market.

#### 5. Trading during maintenance windows

Some exchanges restrict trading during certain hours for system maintenance.

**Avoid it:** Know your exchange’s operational hours and avoid placing orders during scheduled downtime.
